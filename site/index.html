Field guide

How to read this atlas.

Independent research atlas built from public aggregate statistics and open GIS. No individual health records. No claim of DoD endorsement.

Methods & data dictionaryDownload provenance
Counts describe burden, not personal risk

The military location tables have no installation population or training-exposure denominators. A large count can reflect population size, training volume, care access or reporting practice. It does not show that a place is less safe, and the map never converts these counts into rates.

Anchors, footprints and grouped places

Each marker is an approximate display anchor for the published reporting area: not a case coordinate. Grey outlines are DoD MIRTA installation boundaries, drawn for orientation; AFHSD counts are tied to where a case was diagnosed or reported, not to a fence line. A grouped area such as “MCB Camp Lejeune / Cherry Point” keeps one count and one marker even when several boundaries are drawn. Every U.S. anchor lies within 15 km of its footprint. MIRTA has no overseas boundaries (Okinawa, Germany, Korea), and Parris Island is not in the extract used, so only Beaufort is outlined for that group.

Japan: a crude rate, precisely labelled

“2025 transports per 100,000 residents (2024 population)” = FDMA ambulance transports for heat illness, 1 May–30 Sep 2025 (Table 4-2) ÷ Statistics Bureau population estimate, 1 Oct 2024 (Table 2) × 100,000. The 65+ rate uses the 65+ population (Table 3). It is not incidence: transports are counted per event by the prefecture's fire services, so repeat transports count again, and the report does not state residence, so visitors are probably included (our inference). A matched 2025 population table was not yet published on the Statistics Bureau site at build time. Rates are not age-adjusted.

Overlapping windows, thresholds and zeros

The 2020–2024 and 2021–2025 military heat tables overlap by four years: they are never added and their difference is not a trend. Installation renamings (Fort Polk → Fort Johnson, Fort Hood → Fort Cavazos) are kept as each table printed them. Eighteen cold locations are known only to have at least 25 cases over five seasons; they appear as hollow rings, are excluded from totals and the concentration display, and are never treated as 25 or zero. In India's state counts, a recorded zero is a recorded zero, not proof of no heat deaths; union territories are not mapped.

Temperature layers are context, not cause

The NASA layer is MODIS Terra monthly daytime land-surface temperature (GIBS layer MODIS_Terra_L3_Land_Surface_Temp_Monthly_Day, Web Mercator, monthly from March 2000): the skin temperature of the ground, often much hotter than the air. Transparent areas are no data. Field notes add ERA5 reanalysis air-temperature statistics for the ~25 km grid cell nearest each military anchor. None of these is WBGT, the measure behind military heat flags, and none describes conditions when a case occurred.

Recorded and modeled stay apart

Military diagnoses, civilian ambulance transports, recorded deaths and modeled attributable deaths are separate datasets with separate legends. The atlas never sums across them, never interpolates between them, and never applies an “undercount multiplier”. The 2023 DoD finding that 52.6% of diagnosed heat-stroke encounters had no matching reportable-event record describes one reporting system, not cases missing from every system.

Known gaps

Named military locations only, not every installation; 573 overseas heat cases (2021–2025) have no published place and are not mapped. U.S. county/state civilian outcomes from CDC Tracking are not imported: its public API answered some requests and throttled others (“too many non-token requests”) during the build, and its documentation could not be retrieved to confirm token terms, so no partial extract was published. Civilian cold outcomes, India denominators and monthly 2026 military reports are not yet included.

Open tools and data

MapLibre GL JS (BSD-3) on OpenFreeMap vector tiles (OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL); Natural Earth boundaries (public domain; de facto boundaries, no position on disputed territory); U.S. Census cartographic boundaries; DoD MIRTA via the Esri U.S. Federal Datasets mirror; NASA GIBS; ERA5 (Copernicus, CC BY 4.0) via the Open-Meteo API. The Python pipeline records every download's URL, retrieval date and SHA-256 checksum.
